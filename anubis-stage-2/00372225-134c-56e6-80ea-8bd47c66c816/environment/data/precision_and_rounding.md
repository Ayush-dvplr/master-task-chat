# Rounding and precision

All arithmetic is exact integer or fixed point. The fixed-point form is signed Q16.16: a value is held as an integer equal to that value times 65536. A whole number enters the form shifted left by sixteen bits. A basis-point figure enters it as the figure times 65536 divided by ten thousand, and a milli figure as the figure times 65536 divided by one thousand, each rounded half up away from zero. The product of two fixed-point values is their integer product shifted right by sixteen bits, truncating toward zero when the product is negative. The square root of a fixed-point value is the floored integer square root of that value times 65536, and zero for a value at or below zero. Round half up away from zero to a whole fixed-point value at the method stage, the recalibration stage, and the reweight stage. A fixed-point value becomes a whole result by truncating toward zero. Carrying full precision and rounding once at the end is wrong and will disagree with the reference.
