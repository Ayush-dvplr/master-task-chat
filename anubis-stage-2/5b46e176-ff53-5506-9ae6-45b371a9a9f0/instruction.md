A transit interchange has finished collecting one revenue epoch and now has to clear it. Taps from a set of member operators landed in a clearing area during the epoch, and the interchange owes its members a cleared account of that epoch: which taps it priced, which it let go and why, which gates it stopped trusting, what the epoch earned, how that splits across the parts of the estate, what each member owes each other member once the mutual claims are cancelled down, and a proof that nothing was lost or invented along the way. Your job is to produce that cleared account.

You have 7200 seconds to complete this task.

Everything you need is under `/app/data`. There you will find the machine readable registers the interchange keeps, a reference set of rulebooks written for the people who run it, a statement of the invariants every cleared epoch has to satisfy, a calibration slice with the figures a small epoch already cleared to, and one complete artifact the interchange produced for that small epoch. Read all of it before you write anything. The rulebooks are filed the way a reference set is filed, grouped by subsystem and shelved under their own initials, which is not the order in which the rules bear on a tap. Working out the order in which they bear on a tap is part of the job. Each rulebook opens by naming what it consumes and what it produces, and the calibration figures settle what is left.

Seven properties of this interchange have to be recovered rather than read off. They are the order in which the four rate adjustments compose, the quantity the shortfall levy is a share of, the spacing of the settlement grid, the convention the interchange uses for a value that falls exactly between two grid points, the reach of a reversal record, the order in which revenue caps release surplus, and the convention by which a fault window is read at its own two endpoints. Exactly one combination of those seven reproduces every figure published for the calibration slice. Find it, use it, and report it.

Write everything into `/app/out`, and do not modify anything under `/app/data`.

Deliver a program at `/app/out/clear_epoch.py`. It takes two positional arguments, a directory of registers and a directory to write into, and it clears whatever epoch it is given. It must run on Python 3.12 with nothing but the standard library, must finish an epoch this size well inside 300 seconds, and must carry no value that is true only of this particular epoch. Anything it needs about the interchange it has to recover from the registers it was pointed at, because it will be run against other epochs. A register directory holds the machine readable registers and the calibration material and nothing else: the rulebooks, the invariant statement and the example artifact are written for you rather than for the program, so do not expect them beside the registers it is handed. Those registers state their rows in no fixed order, and every figure the interchange parameterises itself with sits in one of them, so the account a register directory produces has to be a function of what its rows say and never of the order they arrive in or of what this epoch happened to publish. The program will be handed epochs whose calendars, policies, tariff tables, faults, exceptions, caps, ceilings and taps all differ from these, including epochs that price nothing at all, and it has to close each of them.

Running that program against `/app/data` has to produce the cleared epoch. `/app/out/summary.json` is the account of the epoch as a whole, and it carries an object named `resolved_parameters` holding the seven properties you recovered, under the key names and with the legal values the calibration material sets out. `/app/out/reconciliation.json` carries the conservation identities and their residuals. `/app/out/tap_ledger.json` carries the tap by tap ledger. `/app/out/data_quality.json` carries what the epoch noticed about its own inputs. `/app/out/identity_register.json` carries the identity work behind the ledger. `/app/out/suspension_register.json` carries the gate, zone and operator states the suspension rules settled on. `/app/out/cap_relief.json` carries the lending between zones and the curtailment it could not avoid.

Alongside those, write one report for every operator that is in scope for this epoch and one for every zone that is in scope, into the same directory, named as the partition rulebook says partition reports are named. The registers this epoch ships admit ten candidate partition reports, and the naming rule puts them at `/app/out/operator_crossline.json`, `/app/out/operator_northline.json`, `/app/out/operator_southline.json`, `/app/out/zone_alder.json`, `/app/out/zone_bramble.json`, `/app/out/zone_cinder.json`, `/app/out/zone_dovecote.json`, `/app/out/zone_elmgate.json`, `/app/out/zone_foundry.json` and `/app/out/zone_gantry.json`. Which of those ten are in scope is a consequence of how the epoch clears, so the set you write is a result rather than a given, and it is published nowhere. Do not assume it, derive it. Leave every candidate that clears out of scope uncreated: a report that should not exist is as wrong as one that is missing.

This epoch has three outcomes of equal standing, and it is closed only when all three are right. The first is the cleared account above. The second is the netting layer the reference set carries in a rulebook filed under N, which reads the cleared ledger and cancels the mutual claims between operators down to a cycle free residual, then schedules what is left against each payer's funding ceiling. The third is the assurance layer, filed under A after admission, which settles the cleared ledger onto a per distance unit basis. Neither of the last two is an appendix to the clearance and neither is optional: each carries its own six artifacts, its own rules and its own conventions, and a correct clearance with a wrong netting or assurance layer is an unclosed epoch in exactly the way a wrong clearance is. Run them after the clearance because they consume the cleared ledger, not because they matter less.

Write the netting account to `/app/out/netting_summary.json`, the gross obligations to `/app/out/netting_obligations.json`, the cycle free residual and the cancellations that produced it to `/app/out/netting_residual.json`, the per operator positions to `/app/out/netting_positions.json`, the funding schedule to `/app/out/netting_schedule.json`, and the cancellation rendering to `/app/out/netting_cycles.csv`. Write the assurance account to `/app/out/assurance_summary.json`, the per tap margins to `/app/out/assurance_margins.json`, the taps it held back to `/app/out/assurance_holdbacks.json`, the ranked order the digest is taken over to `/app/out/assurance_order.json`, the distance apportionment to `/app/out/assurance_apportionment.json`, and the per group rendering to `/app/out/assurance_ladder.csv`. The artifact schema fixes the key names, the row shapes and the CSV headers of all twelve; emit the figures under the names it states.

Those two rulebooks do not reuse the conventions of the rulebooks above them. Take the cycle order, the funding order, the one division that truncates, the estimator the holdback rule uses, the sample size it never holds back from, the median convention for an even sample, and the order the assurance digest is taken over from the rulebook that states them rather than from the ones they sit on top of. Both layers have thresholds that are crossed only by a value standing strictly above them, so a value sitting exactly on a threshold falls on the retaining side.

The artifact the interchange produced for the calibration slice is the authority on the form every artifact takes, down to the byte. No prose anywhere describes that form. Match the example.

When you are done, tell me in your final message the charge the epoch cleared, how many taps it priced, withdrew and put out of epoch, the distance units it priced, the gates it suspended with the reason for each, the zones it left unvalidated, the operators it stood down, the operators and zones that ended in scope, and each of the seven recovered properties quoted exactly as you wrote them into the summary. Say what the conservation residuals came to.

Report the other two layers in that same message: the gross obligation total, every cycle the netting layer cancelled with its sequence and amount, the residual total, each operator's net position with a confirmation that they sum to zero, the netting digest, and which scheduled edges were funded and which deferred. For the assurance layer give the sample size, the median, the dispersion and the threshold, the retained and held back counts, the pool, the digest, and the share each rider class took with a confirmation that the shares sum to the retained distance units. Show the arithmetic for one tap the holdback rule retained and one it held back.

Everything graded here has to come from what is under `/app/data`. No outside source has this interchange or this epoch in it.
