# Zone rules and wall clock resolution

The zone file named by the index (role `zones`) is iCalendar text holding one
`VTIMEZONE` component per zone. A zone may carry any number of `STANDARD` and
`DAYLIGHT` subcomponents and every one of them contributes onsets. Whether a
subcomponent is labelled `STANDARD` or `DAYLIGHT` plays no part in the
arithmetic below; only its offsets and onsets do.

For each subcomponent, the onset set is the `DTSTART` wall clock value, plus
every wall clock value generated by its `RRULE`, plus every wall clock value
listed by an `RDATE` property. An `RRULE` inside a `VTIMEZONE` subcomponent uses
`FREQ=YEARLY` and selects its days with the yearly rules of recurrence-rules.md, so a day
can come from an ordinal `BYDAY` token such as `2SU` or `-1SU`, from a
`BYMONTHDAY` list intersected with a bare `BYDAY` weekday, or from a
`BYYEARDAY` or `BYWEEKNO` list. The time of day of every generated onset is the
time of day of the subcomponent `DTSTART`. When
the rule carries `UNTIL`, the bound is an absolute UTC instant and it is
inclusive; an onset whose UTC instant is later than the bound is not
generated. Onsets earlier than the subcomponent `DTSTART` are not generated.

Each onset is converted to an absolute instant with

    onset_utc = onset_local minus TZOFFSETFROM

Every onset from every subcomponent of a zone is merged into one transition
list. Duplicate transitions collapse. The list is sorted by `onset_utc`.

The offset in force at an absolute instant is the `TZOFFSETTO` of the latest
transition whose `onset_utc` is less than or equal to that instant. For an
instant earlier than every transition, the offset is the `TZOFFSETFROM` of the
first transition.

Converting a wall clock value to an absolute instant uses the candidate test.
For each distinct offset that appears anywhere in the zone, form the candidate
instant `wall minus offset` and keep the candidate when the offset in force at
that candidate equals the offset used to form it.

Exactly one surviving candidate means the wall clock value is `unique`. The
recorded shift is zero.

Two surviving candidates mean the wall clock value is `ambiguous` because a
backward transition repeats it. The earlier of the two absolute instants is
selected, which is the reading that keeps the pre transition offset. The
recorded shift is zero.

No surviving candidate means the wall clock value is `nonexistent` because a
forward transition skips it. Locate the transition whose gap, defined as
`TZOFFSETTO minus TZOFFSETFROM`, is positive and whose interval
`[onset_utc plus TZOFFSETFROM, onset_utc plus TZOFFSETTO)` contains the wall
clock value. Move the wall clock value forward by the gap and resolve it with
the post transition offset. The recorded shift is the gap in minutes.

Converting an absolute instant back to a wall clock value adds the offset in
force at that instant.

A zone is named by its `TZID`. A `TZID` parameter that names no zone directly
is first read through the alias table (zone-aliases.md).

Worked example. The Pacific zone moves from -08:00 to -07:00 at 2027-03-14
10:00Z, the onset 02:00 local minus the -08:00 it moves from. The wall clock
value 2027-03-14 02:30 has no surviving candidate: -08:00 gives 10:30Z, where
-07:00 is in force, and -07:00 gives 09:30Z, where -08:00 is in force. The gap
is 60 minutes, so the value moves to 03:30 and resolves with -07:00 to 10:30Z,
with a recorded shift of 60. On 2027-11-07 the zone moves back from -07:00 to
-08:00 at 09:00Z; the wall clock value 01:30 has two candidates, 08:30Z and
09:30Z, and is ambiguous, so the earlier, 08:30Z, is taken.
