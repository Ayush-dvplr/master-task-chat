# Outside source ledger (searched 31 Oct 2026)

These are primary papers or benchmark owners' reports. Dates are publication or report dates. Each application to Terminal-Bench task authoring is **INFERRED**, not a measured result from our pipeline.

| # | Source and date | What it says | Application to us |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Terminal-Bench 2.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11868), 17 Jan 2026 | 89 curated terminal tasks, each with a human solution and tests; authors emphasize difficulty of validating task correctness and whether all tested properties are specified. | Make oracle, rule trace, and QC proof part of hardness selection; a low score on a broken contract is no win. |
| 2 | [SWE-Bench Pro paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16941), 21 Sep 2025 | Longer, multi-file software tasks and contamination-resistant collection were designed to retain difficulty. | Multi-file work is useful only with fresh unseen behavior and a strong generalization test; breadth by itself leaked locally. |
| 3 | [OpenAI SWE-Bench Pro audit](https://openai.com/index/separating-signal-from-noise-coding-evaluations/), 8 Jul 2026 | Independent audit estimates about 30% of public tasks are broken; visible statement, tests, and gold patch were reviewed separately. | Add a blind contract review before interpreting a failed agent as a genuine miss. |
| 4 | [OpenAI SWE-bench Verified audit](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/), 23 Feb 2026 | Reports task design defects and evidence of training contamination in the established benchmark. | Use newly seeded corpora and avoid public task wording or answer reuse; audit apparent success and failure. |
| 5 | [SWE-bench-Live](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23419), 29 May 2025 | Continually collects fresh executable repository issues to reduce stale-set contamination. | Rotate hidden seeds and task instances while keeping a fixed, published rule space and deterministic scorer. |
| 6 | [SWE-rebench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20411), 26 May 2025 | Continuously extracts diverse real repository tasks for decontaminated evaluation. | Treat freshness as a control, not a hardness lever; locally, reruns were common even in easy tasks. |
| 7 | [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045), 18 Jun 2024 | Stateful conversations, domain policies, API tools, and pass^k expose reliability losses across repeated trials. | A legal stream task can require a solver to preserve state across many ordered events; score several seeded histories separately. |
| 8 | [MLE-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07095), 9 Oct 2024 | 75 competition tasks evaluate full ML engineering workflows against held-out outcomes. | Borrow hidden-instance performance testing, but prefer deterministic exact objectives over noisy training scores in our harness. |
| 9 | [OSWorld 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29537), 28 Jun 2026 | Long workflows stress cross-source reasoning, implicit state, changed information and verification; agent errors include skipped constraints and verification. | Construct a data workflow where later inputs change the meaning of earlier decisions, with every precedence rule stated. |
| 10 | [ARC-AGI-2 technical report](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-agi-2-technical-report), 20 May 2025 | New tasks select for multi-step abstraction and generalization, with human feasibility checks. | A bounded candidate hypothesis space plus separating witness can test inference, but local tariff pilot shows this alone is easy. |
| 11 | [ARC Prize private evaluation guide](https://arcprize.org/guide/1), accessed 31 Oct 2026; page date **UNVERIFIED** | The leaderboard uses private evaluation tasks separate from examples. | Hide instances, not rules; prove the shipped evidence determines every graded property. |
| 12 | [METR time horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons/), updated 8 Sep 2026 | Runs multiple independent attempts; 50% and 80% horizons depend on task duration and agent setup. | Preserve run variance and exact setup; one paid score should not be treated as a universal solve probability. |
| 13 | [METR RE-Bench report](https://metr.org/blog/2024-11-22-evaluating-r-d-capabilities-of-llms/), 22 Nov 2024 | On research engineering tasks, median agents often fail to react to new information or build on progress over time. | Track first wrong assumption and whether later evidence changes the program, not only final score. |
| 14 | [METR HCAST paper](https://metr.org/hcast.pdf), 2025; exact date **UNVERIFIED** | Human reviewers check that agent failures are not caused by missing compute, packages, or unpredictable grading. | Every hidden-scale loss needs a resource and oracle audit before being called difficulty. |
| 15 | [METR SWE-bench merge review](https://metr.org/notes/2026-03-10-many-swe-bench-passing-prs-would-not-be-merged-into-main/), 10 Mar 2026 | Roughly half of test-passing sampled PRs would not be merged by maintainers. | Add outcome and quality checks that measure real behavior; avoid source-token or appearance tests. |
| 16 | [CodeJudgeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10535), 14 Jul 2025 | Code judges show randomness and response-order sensitivity. | Favor deterministic test channel for exact output; keep judge criteria atomic and evidence-linked. |
| 17 | [Rubric position-bias study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02219), 2 Feb 2026 | Rubric score-option order influences judgments; balanced permutations improve agreement with humans. | Audit criterion wording/order on diagnostic copies; do not attribute a rubric shift to task hardness without checking. |
| 18 | [Self-preference in rubric judging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06996), 8 Apr 2026 | Judges can favor outputs from their own model family even with objective binary rubrics. | Use oracle and mutant calibration for every judge criterion; record model family and uncertainty. |
| 19 | [NIST agent-evaluation cheating examples](https://www.nist.gov/caissi/2-examples-cheating-caisis-agent-evaluations), 2025; exact date **UNVERIFIED** | Documents solution lookup, test-specific patches, and evaluator manipulation in agent evaluations. | Keep hidden seeds inaccessible and grade rerunnable behavior; inspect trajectories for leakage rather than trusting a pass. |

## Source limits

- Source 2 describes a harder benchmark, while source 3 later disputes the soundness of many tasks in it. Difficulty claims therefore need task-level QC.
- Sources 7-10 study different agents and interfaces. Transfer to our local Opus pipeline is **INFERRED** until a design blind and paid run reproduce it.
- Source 11 and sources 14 and 19 had no exact page publication date verified in this session; the date is marked above.
