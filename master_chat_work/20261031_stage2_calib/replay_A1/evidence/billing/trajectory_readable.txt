# Concierge desk billing standard

This standard governs one billing cycle of the concierge desk. Its sections are in alphabetical order. The meaning of every column it relies on is given in the record dictionary, and the shape of every report is given in the report layout.

## Allowance credit

Each client's plan includes a number of minutes for the cycle. The included minutes are credited against the client's eligible closings, beginning with the eligible closing whose completion instant is the latest and moving to earlier completion instants. Among eligible closings of one client with the same completion instant, the closing whose closing reference comes first in code point order is credited first. Each closing in turn is credited the smaller of its billable minutes and the included minutes not yet credited. A closing is eligible when it is billed and its tier is one of the credit tiers of the client's plan. The billable minutes of a closing that are not credited are its charged minutes.

## Amendments

An amendment names one closing by handset and handset sequence number. Void and minutes amendments take effect in order of issue instant, and amendments with the same issue instant take effect in code point order of their amendment identifiers. A void or minutes amendment whose issue instant is earlier than the completion instant of the closing it names has no effect, and so has one that names no received closing. A revert names a void or minutes amendment and cancels it, so that the named amendment counts as never issued; a revert whose issue instant is not later than the issue instant of the amendment it names, or that names no void or minutes amendment, has no effect. A closing named by at least one void amendment that has effect and is not cancelled is void. Otherwise, when minutes amendments that have effect and are not cancelled name the closing, its worked minutes are those of the one among them that takes effect last.

## Billable minutes

The billable minutes of a closing are its worked minutes rounded up to the next whole multiple of the billing increment of its service. Worked minutes that are already a whole multiple stay as they are.

## Billed closings

A received closing is billed unless it is rejected, void, outside the cycle or unrated.

## Cycle

The cycle header names the cycle, its first and last local business days, both of them inside the cycle, the rate card in force, and the minimum invoice. A closing whose local business day is earlier than the first day or later than the last day is outside the cycle.

## Duplicates

A closing is identified by its handset and its handset sequence number. When the main export and the legacy export both hold a row with the same identity, the main export row is the closing and the legacy row is a duplicate, which is not a closing at all. Within one export no identity repeats.

## Local business day

The local time of a closing is its completion instant moved by the UTC offset that the zone of its client has in force at that completion instant, as the zone rules give it. The local business day of a closing is the calendar date of its local time.

## Minimum invoice

A region invoice is in scope when the gross amount of the region, as the money rules define gross, is at least the minimum invoice of the cycle header. A segment invoice is in scope when the net amount of the segment is at least that minimum. The region test reads gross amounts and the segment test reads net amounts; each test uses only its own operand.

## Money

Money is carried in integer micro-units, one micro-unit being one millionth of the currency unit. The gross amount of a billed closing is its billable minutes times its rate. The net amount is its charged minutes times its rate. The credit amount is the gross amount minus the net amount. Every total is the sum of the amounts of the closings it covers. The one rounding in this standard turns a net amount in micro-units into whole cents: the amount is divided by 10000 and rounded to the nearest whole cent, an exact half going to the even cent.

## Pricing

The rate of a closing is taken from the rate card that the cycle header names. It is the rate of the row for the closing's service and tier whose effective interval holds the closing's local business day. An effective interval runs from its effective_from day, which it includes, to its effective_to day, which it excludes; an empty effective_to means the interval has no end. A closing without such a row is unrated.

## Rejections

A received closing whose service code is neither a code of the service catalog nor an alias of the alias table is rejected with the reason unknown_service.

## Services

The service of a closing is the catalog code its service code names. A service code that is an alias names the catalog code the alias table maps it to.

## Tiers

The tier of a closing is weekend_holiday when its local business day is a Saturday, a Sunday or a holiday of its client's region. Otherwise it is standard when the time of day of its local time is at or after 08:00:00 and before 18:00:00, and after_hours in every other case.

# Record dictionary

This dictionary gives the layout and the meaning of every input of a billing cycle extract. Files are listed in alphabetical order. The billing rules that use these columns are in the billing standard; the reports are described in the report layout.

## Conventions

Every file is UTF-8 text. A byte order mark at the very start of a file belongs to no column or value. CSV files have one header row, separate fields with commas and may quote a field with double quotes. Every value is read with surrounding spaces removed. Identifiers compare exactly after that, except service codes and alias codes, which compare after conversion to upper case. An instant is written YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS and a day is written YYYY-MM-DD. The exchange clock of the desk keeps UTC, and every instant in this extract is on the exchange clock unless its column says otherwise.

## amendments.jsonl

One JSON object per non-empty line, with the keys amendment, issued_at, action, handset, seq, minutes and reverts. amendment is the amendment identifier. issued_at is the issue instant. action is void, minutes or revert. For void and minutes, handset and seq name the closing (seq is an integer) and reverts is null. For minutes, minutes is the new worked minutes as a positive integer; it is null for the other actions. For revert, handset and seq are null and reverts holds the identifier of the amendment the revert names.

## clients.csv

The client roster, with the columns client, line, segment, region, plan and zone. line is the exchange line the client is reached on; no two clients share a line. segment is one of consumer, corporate, enterprise and smb. region is one of central, east, north, south and west. plan names a row of the plan register and zone names a zone of the zone rules.

## closings.csv

The main export of handset closings, with the columns ref, handset, seq, client, service, stamped_at and worked_minutes. ref is the closing reference. handset names the handset that recorded the closing and seq is its handset sequence number, an integer. client names the client served and service is the service code as recorded on the handset. worked_minutes is a positive integer. stamped_at is the instant on the handset clock at which the assistant closed the task. A handset clock is set when the handset is issued to an assistant and is never adjusted afterwards, so each handset differs from the exchange clock by one whole number of minutes, its clock offset: the exchange clock reading minus the handset clock reading, from -19 to 19. The completion instant of a closing is its stamped_at moved onto the exchange clock by adding the clock offset of its handset.

## code_aliases.csv

The alias table, with the columns alias and service. Each alias is an older service code and service is the catalog code it maps to.

## cycle.json

The cycle header, one JSON object with the keys cycle, first_day, last_day, rate_card and minimum_invoice_micro. cycle is the cycle name. first_day and last_day are days. rate_card is the file name of the rate card in force. minimum_invoice_micro is an integer amount in micro-units.

## exchange_register.csv

The traffic register of the exchange, with the columns day, slot, line and calls. Every received closing makes one closing call on the line of its client at its completion instant. The day of a call is the date of its completion instant, and its slot is the minute of that day holding the completion instant: the slot written HH:MM starts at that time of day, includes its start and ends one minute later, excluding its end. A row gives the number of calls registered on one line in one slot of one day, as a positive integer. The exchange can lose a call, and a lost call is counted in no row; a row never counts more calls than the closings of that line whose completion instants fall in that slot. A slot of a line with no registered call has no row.

## holidays.csv

Regional holidays, with the columns region, day and name. A holiday applies to the clients of its region on that day.

## legacy_closings.txt

The legacy export of handset closings. Lines that start with # are header lines, and blank lines carry nothing. Every other line is one closing with seven fields separated by the character |, in this order: ref, handset, seq, client, service, stamped and duration. The first five fields mean what the same columns of the main export mean. stamped is the instant on the handset clock written DD.MM.YYYY HH:MM:SS, and it plays the part of stamped_at. duration is a positive integer in the unit named by the header line that begins # duration unit: followed by seconds or minutes. A duration in minutes is the worked minutes. A duration in seconds becomes worked minutes when divided by 60 and rounded up to a whole minute. The received closings of an extract are every row of the main export and every legacy row that is not a duplicate.

## plans.csv

The plan register, with the columns plan, included_minutes and credit_tiers. included_minutes is a non-negative integer. credit_tiers lists the credit tiers of the plan as tier names separated by single spaces.

## Rate cards

A rate card is a CSV file with the columns service, tier, rate_micro_per_minute, effective_from and effective_to. service is a catalog code, tier is a tier name and rate_micro_per_minute is a positive integer in micro-units per billable minute. effective_from is a day and effective_to is a day or empty. The rows of one rate card for one service and tier have effective intervals that do not overlap.

## service_catalog.csv

The service catalog, with the columns service, product_line and increment_minutes. service is the catalog code, product_line names the product line of the service, and increment_minutes is the billing increment as a positive integer.

## zone_rules.csv

The zone rules, with the columns zone, from_utc and utc_offset_minutes. A row gives the UTC offset in minutes that the zone keeps from its from_utc instant, which it includes, until the from_utc instant of the next row of the same zone. Local time is the exchange clock reading plus the UTC offset in force.

# Report layout

This layout gives the form of every report of a billing cycle run. Each field takes its meaning from the rule of the billing standard or the column of the record dictionary that it names.

## Byte form

Every report ending in .json holds one JSON object. It is written compactly: no space or line break inside it, the separators , and : with nothing around them, the keys of every object in code point order, and every character outside ASCII written as a \u escape. One line feed follows it, and nothing else. normalized.jsonl holds one such object per line, each followed by one line feed; with no billed closing it is empty. Whole numbers are JSON integers, flags are true or false, and identifiers, instants, days and names are JSON strings. Every list of identifiers is in code point order.

## Standard output

A run prints one line for each report it writes, in code point order of the report file names, and nothing else. Each line is the word wrote, one space, the file name, one space and the size of the written file in bytes as a decimal integer.

## data_quality.json

- amendments_without_effect: the identifiers of every amendment that has no effect under the amendments rule, together with every amendment cancelled by a revert.
- closings_received: the number of received closings.
- duplicates: the closing references of the legacy rows that are duplicates under the duplicates rule.
- input_files: the names of every file in the extract.
- outside_cycle: the closing references of the received closings outside the cycle under the cycle rule, rejected closings left out.
- rejected: an object with one key per rejected closing reference, whose value is the rejection reason.
- unrated: the closing references of the closings that are unrated under the pricing rule, rejected, void and outside-cycle closings left out.
- void: the closing references of the void closings under the amendments rule, rejected and outside-cycle closings left out.

## normalized.jsonl

One object per billed closing, in order of completion instant and, for equal completion instants, in code point order of closing reference.

- billable_minutes: the billable minutes.
- charged_minutes: the charged minutes under the allowance credit rule.
- client, handset, ref: as recorded.
- completed_at: the completion instant.
- credited_minutes: the minutes credited under the allowance credit rule.
- gross_micro, net_micro: the gross and net amounts under the money rules.
- line, region, segment: those of the client in the client roster.
- local_day: the local business day.
- product_line: the product line of the service.
- rate_micro_per_minute: the rate under the pricing rule.
- seq: the handset sequence number, as an integer.
- service: the service, as its catalog code.
- tier: the tier.
- worked_minutes: the worked minutes after the amendments rule.

## product_summary.json

- product_lines: a list with one object per product line of the service catalog, in code point order of product line, each holding billable_minutes, closings, gross_micro, net_micro and product_line. closings counts the billed closings of the line and the other figures total them.

## region_central.json, region_east.json, region_north.json, region_south.json, region_west.json

One report per region, each written whatever its scope.

- by_tier: an object with one key per tier of at least one billed closing of the region, whose value is the net amount of those closings.
- clients: the clients with at least one billed closing in the region.
- closings: the number of billed closings of the region.
- credit_micro, gross_micro, net_micro: the totals of the billed closings of the region.
- in_scope: the region test of the minimum invoice rule.
- net_cents: net_micro in whole cents under the money rules.
- region: the region name.

## segment_consumer.json, segment_corporate.json, segment_enterprise.json, segment_smb.json

One report per segment, each written whatever its scope, with the keys of a region report except that segment, the segment name, takes the place of region, every figure covers the billed closings of the segment, and in_scope is the segment test of the minimum invoice rule.

## summary.json

- by_tier: as in a region report, over every billed closing.
- closings_billed: the number of billed closings.
- credit_micro, gross_micro, net_micro: the totals of every billed closing.
- cycle: the cycle name.
- net_cents: net_micro in whole cents under the money rules.
- reconciliation: an object holding normalized_net_micro, the total of net_micro over normalized.jsonl; product_net_micro, the total over the product lines of product_summary.json; region_net_micro, the total over the five region reports; segment_net_micro, the total over the four segment reports; and residual_micro, which is product_net_micro plus region_net_micro plus segment_net_micro minus three times normalized_net_micro.

## The program

`/app/out/transform.py` (standard library only, ~0.3 s per run) reads a cycle extract and writes the 13 reports. The one thing not stated in the inputs is each handset's clock offset: it is inferred from `exchange_register.csv` — every received closing makes one call on its client's line in the minute slot of its completion instant, rows are lower bounds (calls can be lost) — by taking the offsets that leave the fewest registered calls unexplained, scored over the whole register (so lines served by two handsets count too).
