Instruction anatomy (instruction.md, 37 lines, 779 words; ours 448 / 571 / 350 / 408).
- Opening is a short story: "A country grain elevator kept a small program called the settlement engine ... When the scale house was recabled the engine was lost, and the firmware that held its constants went with it. What survives is in `/app/data`." (:1).
- What the docs do and do not give (:3): file shapes, screening, carried state, stage list, status ladder, report keys, byte forms, the two digests; "They give none of the numbers, and they do not say what any bit of the intake's mode word does."
- Evidence and method are stated outright. (:5) the two mode-zero intakes "are the evidence for the plain chain: the normalisation factor, the per commodity and per contract adjustments, the band edges, the role tolerances, the dockage charge and its ceiling, the disposition thresholds and the two tie breaks can all be pinned down from them" (= a list of every constant to fit). (:7) the graded intake has "all thirteen bits set"; "Those digests are how a reading of the mode word is settled: resolve the intake under a candidate reading, digest the result, and compare. A wrong reading of any bit moves at least one bin digest, so the search is decidable one bin at a time rather than a guess." = number of hidden items (13) + search procedure + decidability claim.
- Deliverable (:9-15): one program `/app/out/settle.py`, args input dir then output dir, writes three named reports "in the exact form `/app/data/reports.md` describes"; reads the mode word from the manifest and honours it "because it will be driven over intakes you have never seen, at mode words you have never seen" (hidden-input generalisation); stdlib only, no precomputed answers, byte-identical reruns, `/app/data` read only.
- Closing report (:17-33): eleven named integers, each defined in one line (e.g. "capped_tickets how many resolved tickets carry a dockage_before sitting exactly at the dockage ceiling"), plus "how many of the thirteen mode bits you settled and which ones are still open", bins count, tickets count, whether every bin digest and the intake digest agree.
- Strategy sentence (:35): "A partial engine that is written down and reported on is worth more than a perfect one that never gets written, so put the program in place and give the closing report before you spend whatever time is left on the bits you are still unsure of." (judge R22 rewards exactly this).
- Procedure vs end state: mixed. End state for the program; explicit method for the hard part (digest search, per bin) and explicit ordering advice (deliver early). Tone narrative, plain, generous.
Side by side with ours:
- Closest to emberforge: ours says "Four conventions ... only the register of candidates is published. Exactly one draw from that register reproduces what the council published from the calibration slice" (emberforge instruction.md:5); this one says "thirteen bits" and "resolve ... digest ... compare" (:7). Both give count + verification oracle. The difference: emberforge gives a CLOSED candidate register (finite choice, 1 of N); this task gives NO candidate space - the alterations have to be invented (agent's searches used 20+ free knobs, trajectory steps 22, 34, 36).
- Versus quillfen (task 05): this prompt is far more procedural (method, count, ordering advice), and still scored 24 %.
Evidence on the operator hypothesis: this task does NOT support "over-specified instructions produce high scores": it tells the method and the count and scores 6 % tests / 42 % rubric. But the dominant causes are (a) an open-ended hypothesis space with only a hash oracle, and (b) the unidentifiable bit-position mapping behind 78/94 of the test weight (step 2). So it shows that telling the method is not enough when the search space is open and the oracle is a digest; it does not show that precision is harmless.
